\abstract{The combined impact of the number of categories ($k$), sample size ($n$), and the severity of non-normality on normality tests of the composite score in Likert scales has been scarcely systematized. Through Monte Carlo simulation (10,000 replications) and subsampling of six real instruments, we evaluated how $k$ (3 to 9), $n$, and the underlying distribution interact in the power of 11 tests. Under the nominal criterion ($p < .05$), power declines slightly as $k$ grows. However, 8 of the 11 tests reject up to 100\% of the time without real severity due to discretization. When the threshold miscalibration is corrected, the pattern reverses: adjusted power is higher at large $k$ (gap between $-.15$ and $-.37$), consistent with the empirical evidence. Only D'Agostino-Pearson, Jarque-Bera, and the kurtosis test show the smallest deviation from the nominal 5\%.}
